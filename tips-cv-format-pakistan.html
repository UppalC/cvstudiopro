CV format in Pakistan

Many Pakistani CVs carry a lot of personal details. Here is a clear order to follow, what employers commonly expect, and which details you should think twice about sharing.

Updated November 2026 · Written by the CV Studio Pro team · All CV tips

There is no single official format

No law in Pakistan says what a private-sector CV must look like. Habits differ between industries, cities and companies. Banks, factories, schools, hospitals and call centres may all expect slightly different things. When the job advertisement gives instructions, follow them exactly. This guide describes common practice and good judgment, not a legal rule.

A simple order that works

  1. Name and contact: full name, mobile number, email and city.
  2. Short summary: two or three lines about your field and strongest skills.
  3. Work experience: newest job first, with job title, company, city and dates, then short bullet points.
  4. Education: degree, institute, board or university, and year. Freshers can put this before experience.
  5. Skills: only skills you can really use, for example MS Excel, Urdu typing, QuickBooks, customer handling.
  6. Certificates, languages and references: keep them short. “References available on request” is enough.

Personal details: what is common and what is wise

Pakistani CVs often include a photo, father’s name, date of birth, CNIC number, domicile, religion and marital status. Employers may ask for these, but none of them makes your CV better, and some can lead to unfair judgment. A sensible approach:

  • Photo: include a clean, passport-style photo only if the employer or the type of job (for example front-desk or sales) expects it. Never use a selfie or a heavily edited picture.
  • CNIC number: do not put it on a CV that you send to many people or post online. It is an identity number. Give it only when a real employer needs it, for example after an interview or on an official form.
  • Father’s name, domicile, religion: add them only if the form or the employer specifically asks.
  • Date of birth and marital status: optional in most cases. Add them if the advertisement requests them.

Government and public jobs

Many government and public-sector posts are applied for through a prescribed form, an online portal or a set of attested documents, and the form decides which details you give. Follow the advertisement and the portal instructions, and keep your CV for the extra copy that you attach.

Length and style

  • One page for freshers, up to two pages for experienced people.
  • One clear font in black or dark blue, with plain headings. Avoid coloured backgrounds, crowded tables and too many photos or logos.
  • Write in English unless the job asks for Urdu. Ask someone to check your spelling.
  • Save as PDF with a clear name, for example Ali-Raza-CV.pdf.

Common mistakes

  • Using a childish or joking email address.
  • Writing the same duties for every job instead of what you achieved.
  • Leaving unexplained date gaps, or inflating a job title.
  • Putting a long personal statement about “hard work and honesty” with no proof.
