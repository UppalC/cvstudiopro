How to write a cover letter

A cover letter is a short message that goes with your CV. It tells the employer why you are applying and why you fit the job.

Updated November 2026 · Written by the CV Studio Pro team · All CV tips

Do you need one?

Not every employer asks for a cover letter. If the job advertisement asks for one, send it. If it does not mention it, a short, well-written cover letter (or a short message in the email body) can still help, especially for jobs where communication matters. Never send a cover letter that is copied from the internet without changes.

The structure: four short parts

  1. Greeting. Use the person’s name if the advertisement gives it: “Dear Mr. Khan”. If not, “Dear Hiring Manager” is fine.
  2. Opening (1 to 2 sentences). Say which job you are applying for and where you saw it.
  3. Middle (one short paragraph, or two). Explain why you fit. Pick two or three things from the job advertisement and match them with your real experience or skills. Give one concrete example.
  4. Closing (1 to 2 sentences). Thank the reader, say you have attached your CV and that you would be glad to discuss the job in an interview. Add your phone number.

Example you can adapt

Dear Hiring Manager,

I am applying for the Computer Operator position advertised on your website. I have three years of office experience in data entry, records and daily reporting.

In my current role I enter and check about 150 customer records every day and prepare weekly summary sheets in MS Excel for my manager. I am careful with details and comfortable meeting daily deadlines, which I understand are important for this role.

I have attached my CV. I would be glad to discuss how I can help your team. You can reach me on 0300-1234567.

Yours sincerely,
Ali Raza

This is only an example. Change the job title, the facts and the numbers so that every sentence is true for you.

Tips

  • Keep it to one page, and ideally under 250 words.
  • Do not repeat your whole CV. Add what the CV does not say: why this job, why this company.
  • Use the company’s name and the job title correctly. Check spelling twice.
  • Use a polite, simple tone. Avoid long, heavy words.
  • Send it as a PDF or in the body of the email, as the employer asks.

Common mistakes

  • The same letter for every job, with another company’s name left in by mistake.
  • Starting every sentence with “I”.
  • Claiming skills you do not have.
  • Spelling and grammar errors in the first line.
