CV format for the UAE and the Gulf

Gulf employers often look for different details than employers in the UK or Canada. Here is what the main guides agree on, and where they disagree.

Updated November 2026 · Written by the CV Studio Pro team · All CV tips

Mukhtasar: Gulf ki CV 1 se 2 page ki rakhein. Nationality aur visa status zaroor likhein. Photo aksar chalti hai lekin har company ke liye zaroori nahi. Documents ki copies sath na lagayein.

Start with the employer

The Gulf is not one market. Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh, Doha and Muscat all have different company cultures, and a bank, a construction company and a hospital will want different things. Read the advertisement and the company website first. This page gives general guidance from public career guides, and none of it is a legal requirement.

Length and layout

Keep your CV to one or two pages, even if you have many years of experience. Use a clean, single-column layout with standard headings so that online application systems can read it. See what an ATS is, and try our free ATS CV checker.

Details that Gulf employers often want

  • Nationality: Gulf CV guides commonly list it under personal details. If you hold two nationalities, say so.
  • Visa or residence status: if you are already in the UAE, write whether you are on a visit visa, an employment visa or a dependent visa. For Saudi Arabia some guides suggest a line such as “Iqama status: transferable” when it applies to you.
  • Availability: employers often prefer candidates who can join quickly. State your notice period honestly, or “available immediately” if it is true.
  • Phone with country code: for example +971 or +92, so that a recruiter can call you or message you on WhatsApp.
  • Languages: list English and Arabic with an honest level. Do not call yourself fluent if you are not.
  • Licences and certificates: a valid driving licence (especially for sales or driving jobs), safety courses, trade certificates and international certifications, with year and issuer.

The photo question

This is where guides disagree. GulfTalent says most Dubai companies prefer a photo, but it is less common with multinationals and senior roles, and it matters most in customer-facing jobs. A Saudi CV guide we read says a photo is common but not mandatory, and sources for Saudi Arabia are not consistent. Our advice: check the advertisement, and when you add a photo, use a plain, professional, passport-style picture. Never use a selfie. For a technical or senior role at an international company, you can often leave it out.

Do not attach copies of documents

Do not attach your passport, certificates or other documents to a first application. Provide them only when an employer asks. This also protects your personal information.

Write achievements and explain your background

  • Use short bullet points with results, such as “Handled 40 customer calls a day”. Use real numbers only.
  • Explain lesser-known employers and universities in a few words, for example “ABC Ltd, a textile exporter in Faisalabad, Pakistan”.
  • If you have Gulf experience, make it easy to see: employer, city and dates.

Recruiter scams

A real employer does not charge you for an interview or a job. Be careful about any agent who asks for money first, and check an overseas job offer through official channels before you pay or share documents. More in CV for jobs abroad.

Sources

These public guides informed this page: GulfTalent, Dubai jobs guide: building your profile and VisualCV, Saudi Arabian CV guide. Practices change, so check each employer’s instructions.
