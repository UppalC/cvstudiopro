How to write a CV for jobs abroad

A CV for a job overseas is similar to a local one, but employers abroad often expect a cleaner layout and less personal detail. Here is what to check.

Updated November 2026 · Written by the CV Studio Pro team · All CV tips

Mukhtasar: Bahar ki job ke liye CV chhoti aur saaf rakhein. Personal details sirf wohi dein jo employer maange. Har mulk aur company ke rules alag ho sakte hain.

Check the rules for that country and employer first

There is no single “international” CV. Expectations differ between countries and even between companies. Before you send anything, read the job advertisement and the employer’s website carefully. If they give instructions, follow them. This guide gives general ideas, not legal rules for any one country.

Keep the layout simple

Use a clear, one or two page layout with standard headings: Summary, Experience, Education, Skills. Many overseas employers receive applications through online systems, so a plain layout is safer. See what an ATS is for details.

Personal details: less is often better

In many countries employers do not expect a photo, date of birth, marital status, religion or a father’s name on a CV, and some prefer that you leave them out. In other places, agencies or employers may ask for some of these. The safest approach: include only what the advertisement or recruiter asks for.

Do not post your CNIC or passport number on a CV that you send widely. Give identity numbers only when the employer or agency needs them for a real hiring step.

Useful things to include

  • City and country you live in, and your phone number with the country code (for Pakistan, +92).
  • Nationality and languages, with an honest level (for example English: good, Arabic: basic).
  • Work authorisation or notice period, if relevant: for example, “available to join within 30 days”.
  • Licences or certificates that matter for the trade, such as a driving licence, a trade certificate or a safety course, with the issuing body and year.

Write achievements, not only duties

Overseas employers often compare many applications quickly. Short bullet points with results are easier to judge than a long list of responsibilities. Use numbers only when they are true.

Explain your qualifications clearly

Write degree names in full and add the country, for example “BS Computer Science, University of the Punjab, Pakistan, 2022”. Employers in another country may not know your institute, so clear names help them.

Be careful with recruiters and agents

A genuine employer should not ask you to pay money to be considered for a job. Be careful with any offer that asks for fees before an interview, and verify the company through official sources before sharing documents or paying anyone. If you are unsure, check with the relevant government office for overseas employment.

Country guides

For details by country, read CV format in Pakistan, CV format for the UAE and the Gulf, UK CV format and Canadian resume format.

Final checklist

  • Spelling in English checked by a second person.
  • Dates correct and without gaps you cannot explain.
  • Phone number and email correct, with a professional email address.
  • File saved as PDF (unless Word is requested) with a clear name such as Ali-Raza-CV.pdf.
