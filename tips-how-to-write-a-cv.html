How to write a CV, step by step

A good CV is short, clear and easy to scan. This guide shows what to put in, in what order, and what to leave out.

Updated November 2026 · Written by the CV Studio Pro team · All CV tips

Mukhtasar: CV chhoti, saaf aur scan karne mein asaan honi chahiye. Sab se naya kaam sab se upar likhein aur nateeje likhein, sirf duties nahi.

1. Know what a CV is for

A CV does one job: it helps an employer decide, in a few seconds, whether to call you. It is not your life story. Everything you write should help answer one question: why should we talk to this person for this job?

2. Start with the right contact details

At the top put your full name, a job title that matches the work you want, a phone number, a professional email address and your city. An email such as firstname.lastname@gmail.com looks better than a nickname. A LinkedIn link is optional.

Photo, date of birth, CNIC number, marital status and father’s name are common in some local CVs, but they are optional. Add them only if the job advertisement or the employer asks for them. For jobs abroad or with international companies, many employers prefer that you leave them out.

3. Write a short summary

Under your name write two or three lines: who you are, how many years of experience you have (or what you studied if you are new), and your strongest skills. Keep it specific.

Weak: Hard-working person looking for a good opportunity in a reputed company.
Better: Computer operator with 3 years of office experience in data entry, MS Excel reporting and customer records. Fast, accurate and comfortable working to daily deadlines.

4. List your experience, newest first

For each job write the job title, company name, city and dates. Under it add 3 to 5 short bullet points. Start each point with an action word and, where you honestly can, add a number or a result.

Duty only: Responsible for entering data.
Result: Entered and checked about 150 customer records per day with very few corrections.

Only use numbers that are true. If you do not have a figure, describe the result in words instead.

5. Add education and training

Write your degree or certificate, the institute and the year. If you are an experienced worker, keep education short and put it after experience. If you are a fresh graduate, you can put education first. Add short courses and certificates that are relevant to the job.

6. Choose skills that match the job

Read the job advertisement and list the skills it asks for, but only those you really have. Group them simply, for example Software, Technical and Languages. Avoid rating yourself with bars or percentages for every skill; they say little to an employer.

7. Keep it to one or two pages

If you have under ten years of experience, aim for one page. Two pages are fine for longer careers. Use a readable font size, clear headings and enough white space. A crowded page is harder to read than a slightly longer one.

8. Check before you send

  • Read it aloud once to catch spelling and grammar mistakes.
  • Check that phone number and email are correct.
  • Make sure dates are in order and do not overlap by mistake.
  • Save it as a PDF so the layout does not change on the employer’s device, unless the job ad asks for a Word file.
  • Name the file clearly, for example Ali-Raza-CV.pdf.

Common mistakes

  • Using one CV for every job without changing the summary and skills.
  • Writing long paragraphs instead of short bullet points.
  • Too many colours, fonts or graphics.
  • Including unrelated old jobs in detail.
  • Untrue claims. Employers often check, and it can cost you the job later.
