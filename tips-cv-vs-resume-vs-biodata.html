CV vs resume vs biodata: what is the difference?

People use these three words in different ways. Here is a clear explanation and how to choose the right one for your application.

Updated November 2026 · Written by the CV Studio Pro team · All CV tips

Mukhtasar: Pakistan mein aam tor par CV aur resume aik hi cheez samjhe jate hain. Asal farq mulk aur job ke hisaab se hota hai. Job ad ki hidayat hamesha pehle dekhein.

The short answer

In everyday Pakistani use, “CV” and “resume” usually mean the same thing: a document that lists your education, experience and skills. The strict difference depends on the country and the type of job. A “biodata” is an older style that focuses on personal details.

Resume

A resume is a short summary of your work history and skills made for a specific job. In the United States and Canada this is the normal word, and employers there generally expect one page for early careers and up to two pages for longer ones. It is usually changed for every job you apply to.

CV (curriculum vitae)

In the UK, Europe, Pakistan and many other countries, “CV” is the normal word for the same kind of document, usually one to two pages. In academic and research work, a CV can be much longer, because it lists publications, research, teaching and conferences in full.

Biodata

A biodata is a document that puts personal details first: date of birth, father’s name, marital status, religion, address and so on, followed by education and sometimes a short work history. It is still seen in some local government or traditional job applications and in marriage proposals, but modern employers usually prefer a CV that focuses on skills and achievements. If an employer asks for a biodata, give them what they ask for. If they do not, a CV is normally the better choice.

Quick comparison

  • Resume: short, tailored to the job, common in the US and Canada.
  • CV: one to two pages for most jobs in Pakistan and the UK; longer for academic work.
  • Biodata: personal details first; used for some local or traditional applications.

Which one should you use?

  1. Read the job advertisement. If it says “send your CV” or “resume”, send a one or two page document with your experience and skills.
  2. If it asks for specific details or a particular format, follow that format exactly.
  3. If you are applying abroad, check what that country and employer expect. Rules about photos and personal details differ.
  4. When you are not sure, a clean one-page CV with your contact details, summary, experience, education and skills works for most jobs.
