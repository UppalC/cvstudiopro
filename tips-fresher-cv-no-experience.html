Fresher CV: how to write a CV with no work experience

No job history does not mean an empty CV. Employers hiring freshers look for education, skills, projects and attitude. Here is how to show them.

Updated November 2026 · Written by the CV Studio Pro team · All CV tips

Mukhtasar: Experience na ho to education, projects, internship, courses aur skills ko saaf tareeqe se likhein. Jhoot kabhi na likhein.

What employers expect from a fresher

When you apply for your first job, no one expects years of experience. They want to see that you studied the right subject, have basic skills for the role, and show effort. Your CV should make those things easy to see.

Suggested order

  1. Name and contact details
  2. Short summary (2 to 3 lines)
  3. Education (put it first, because it is your strongest section)
  4. Projects or Internship / training
  5. Skills
  6. Certificates and courses
  7. Activities and volunteering (optional)

Writing the summary

Weak: Fresh graduate seeking a challenging job.
Better: BS Computer Science graduate (2026) with practical experience in web pages, basic databases and MS Office. Completed a final-year inventory project and looking for a junior IT role.

Change the summary for each job so it matches what the employer asks for.

Make education useful

Write your degree, institute and year. If your result is good, add it. Add only the subjects or final-year project that connect to the job. You do not need to list every subject.

Use projects as experience

Class projects, a final-year project, a small website you built, a shop you helped manage, or a community event you organised all show real skills. For each one write what it was, what you did and what the result was.

Example: Final-year project: Built a simple student attendance system with a team of three. I designed the database and the login page. The class teacher used it for one semester.

Include internships, training and part-time work

A short internship, a summer job or helping in a family business is valid experience. Write it like a normal job: title, place, dates and two or three bullet points about what you did. Be honest about the length and your role.

Show skills you can prove

List skills the job needs, such as MS Excel, typing, English communication, basic accounting, or a programming language. Put only skills you could demonstrate if asked in an interview. Add certificates from courses you actually completed.

Things to avoid

  • Claiming experience you do not have.
  • Long personal details that the job does not need.
  • Copying a sample CV word for word. Employers see the same text again and again.
  • Spelling mistakes in the first line. Ask a friend or teacher to read it.
