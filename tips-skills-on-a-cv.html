Which skills to put on a CV, and how to write them

The skills section is easy to write badly. Here is how to choose skills that help your application and how to avoid empty words.

Updated November 2026 · Written by the CV Studio Pro team · All CV tips

Mukhtasar: Sirf wo skills likhein jo job ko chahiye aur jo aap sach mein kar sakte hain. Lambi list ke bajaye 6 se 10 asal skills behtar hain.

Start from the job, not from a long list

Read the job advertisement and underline the skills and tools it asks for. Then check each one honestly: can you do it, or learn it quickly? Put those on your CV, and leave out skills that are not relevant to this job.

Two kinds of skills

Hard skills are things you can learn and test: MS Excel, typing speed, accounting software, a programming language, welding, driving, customer support tools.

Soft skills are about how you work: communication, teamwork, time management, problem solving. They matter, but words like “hard-working” and “team player” prove nothing on their own. It is better to show them through your experience bullets: “Trained two new staff members” shows teamwork better than the word does.

How many skills?

Six to ten well-chosen skills are usually better than twenty. A long list looks like padding, and it makes your best skills harder to spot.

Group them so they scan easily

Software: MS Excel, MS Word, QuickBooks
Technical: Data entry, inventory records, basic bookkeeping
Languages: Urdu (native), English (good), Punjabi (native)

Do not rate every skill with bars or percentages

A bar showing “85%” has no clear meaning, because no one knows what 100% is. If your level matters, use simple honest words: basic, good, advanced. Or skip the level and prove the skill through your experience.

Examples by field (only use what is true for you)

  • Office / admin: MS Office, data entry, filing and records, email handling, report writing.
  • Sales / customer support: customer handling, order processing, phone and WhatsApp support, complaint resolution, basic CRM tools.
  • Accounts: bookkeeping, bank reconciliation, invoicing, payroll basics, accounting software you have actually used.
  • IT / computer repair: hardware diagnosis, Windows installation, networking basics, data backup and recovery, troubleshooting.
  • Teaching: lesson planning, classroom management, assessment, communicating with parents.

Use the same words as the job ad, but only when true

If the advertisement says “MS Excel”, write “MS Excel”, not only “computer skills”. This helps both people and software that searches for those words. Never add a skill you could not demonstrate in an interview.

Back skills up

If a skill is important, show it somewhere else on your CV: in a job bullet, a project or a certificate. A skill that appears with proof is far more believable.
